Design a Board Game

A project guide to creating a fun and effective board game for second language learning. Let's make learning an adventure!

1

First Check: Identify the Challenge

Your Task

  • Brainstorm challenges in learning a second language (e.g., vocabulary, grammar, speaking confidence).

  • Focus on one specific area or challenge your game will address.

  • Come with initial ideas for a game that could tackle this problem.

Expectations

We will finalize the specific language-learning challenge your game will solve, ensuring your project has a clear and focused educational goal.

Game Design Kick-starter

Before you start researching other games, think deeply about your own concept. Answering these questions will help you create a strong outline for your core game mechanics.

The Core Loop (Gameplay):

What does a player do on their turn? (e.g., Roll dice and move? Draw a card? Guess a word?) How does a player make progress toward winning?

The Learning Goal (Education):

How will your game directly address the language challenge you chose? If the challenge is vocabulary, how do players learn new words? If it's grammar, how do they practice it?

The Fun Factor (Engagement):

Why would someone want to play your game again? Is there an element of luck? Strategy? Teamwork? A funny consequence for a wrong answer?

2

Second Check: Research & Core Game Concept

Your Task

  • Research 2-3 existing board games that could be applied to learning.

  • Analyze their mechanics: What makes them fun? How do players win?

  • Develop a core concept for your own game and create a draft outline for your report.

Expectations

You will present your research and a solid concept for your game. We'll discuss how you can transform your inspiration into a truly original idea.

3

Third Check: Rough Draft & Game Prototype

Your Task

  • Write a full rough draft of your report and game rulebook.

  • Create a physical or digital prototype of your game (board, cards, pieces).

  • Your rulebook must define the age group, aims, rules, and components.

Expectations

You will present a playable prototype and a complete draft. We will review the rules for clarity and provide feedback to improve the gameplay and learning value.

4

Fourth Check: Final Report & Presentation Plan

Your Task

  • Revise and edit your report and rulebook into a final version to be handed in.

  • Create a storyboard or plan for your 10-15 minute presentation.

  • Plan how you will demonstrate your game to the class.

Expectations

I will collect your final written report. We will then refine your presentation plan and schedule your final demonstration.

5

Fifth Check: Final Presentation & Game Demo

Your Task

  • Practice your 10-15 minute presentation and game demonstration.

  • Have your final game prototype (the Product) ready to display.

  • Present your work and confidently demonstrate how your game is played.

Expectations

A clear, engaging presentation that explains your design process and a fun demonstration that shows how your game makes language learning effective.

Project Rules & Grading

General Rules

  • No plagiarism! You must reference all sources.
  • Stick to the check days and the deadline (5 check-ins required).
  • Be ready to present your work orally in class.
  • All work must be your own product without outside help.
  • Papers must be presentable, tidy, and well-written (Calibri, 11pt if typed). Submit in a file with your name, number, and class.
  • Presentations may be supported by a sketch, PowerPoint, or iMovie.

Grading Breakdown

  • Report: 50%
  • Product: 25%
  • Presentation: 25%
